P0004	[List Paragraph]	Need for teleoperation in construction

P0006	[List Paragraph]	Role of visual interface design in robot teleoperation
P0007	[List Paragraph]	How the remote scene is displayed and represented shapes the visual and spatial information available to the operator during remote operation.

P0009	[List Paragraph]	Background
P0010	[List Paragraph]	Visual Demands in Teleoperation in Construction
P0011	[List Paragraph]	Teleoperated construction tasks require operators to manipulate tools across spatially extended work areas while remaining physically separated from the work site.
P0012	[List Paragraph]	Visual-interface design consequently shapes the information available for remote control and work monitoring.
P0013	[List Paragraph]	Visual Interface Approaches for Robot Teleoperation
P0014	[List Paragraph]	Robot teleoperation relies on visual interfaces to convey task information.
P0015	[List Paragraph]	Two respects of visual interface: workspace representation and display modality.
P0016	[List Paragraph]	Workspace Representation;
P0017	[List Paragraph]	Pros/Cons of stereo imagery and point cloud reconstruction. [4–6].
P0018	[List Paragraph]	Stereo imagery: Directly preserves the visual appearance of task environment. / Restricted spatial exploration and inspection of work areas.
P0019	[List Paragraph]	PCD: Providing spatial context and access to alternative viewpoints. / Reduced appearance fidelity can obscure task-relevant visual information.
P0020	[List Paragraph]	These characteristics create a trade-off between appearance fidelity and viewpoint flexibility.
P0021	[List Paragraph]	Display Modality;
P0022	[List Paragraph]	Pros/Cons of monitor and VR interfaces. [1–3].
P0023	[List Paragraph]	Monitor: Stable, familiar visual reference in a fixed screen space. / Relatively limited immersion and spatial engagement.
P0024	[List Paragraph]	VR: Provide stereoscopic viewing and can couple the displayed view to head movement. / Physical discomfort, additional workload, and usability challenges.
P0025	[List Paragraph]	These characteristics create a trade-off between spatial engagement and interaction discomfort.
P0026	[List Paragraph]	Problem Statement
P0027	[List Paragraph]	Empirical evidence remains limited on how two respects of visual interfaces affect operator performance and experience in teleoperated construction tasks, such as painting.
P0028	[List Paragraph]	Limited empirical knowledge of what visual information operators need during surface-treatment tasks. [19,20].
P0029	[List Paragraph]	Research Objective
P0030	[List Paragraph]	To compare image-based stereo and reconstructed point-cloud (PCD) representations as two approaches to workspace representation, and monitor and VR as two display modalities for visual-interface design in construction teleoperation.
P0031	[List Paragraph]	Focusing on their effects on task performance and operator experience.
P0032	[List Paragraph]	Scenario
P0033	[List Paragraph]	Remote robotic painting was selected as a representative task for the empirical investigation.
P0034	[List Paragraph]	Characteristics of construction surface treatment tasks, including painting [13–26].
P0035	[List Paragraph]	Research Tasks
P0036	[List Paragraph]	Evaluate four visual-interface conditions formed by two display modalities (Monitor and VR) and two visualization methods (Stereo and PCD): Monitor Stereo, Monitor PCD, VR Stereo, and VR PCD.
P0037	[List Paragraph]	Compare four visual-interface conditions in remote robotic painting using task-performance measures and participant interviews.
P0038	[List Paragraph]	Identify task-grounded visual requirements for surface-treatment teleoperation.
P0039	[List Paragraph]	Contribution
P0040	[List Paragraph]	Comparative evaluation of four visual-interface configurations in a remote robotic painting task.

P0042	[List Paragraph]	Identification of task-grounded visual requirements for remote surface treatment task.

P0142	[Heading 1]	Discussion
P0143	[List Paragraph]	Summary of results
P0144	[List Paragraph]	Effects of Visual-Interface Conditions on Operator Performance and Experience (RQ1; Table. 6)
P0145	[List Paragraph]	Monitor Stereo
P0146	[List Paragraph]	The most preferred condition & stable and intuitive reference for precise task execution.
P0147	[List Paragraph]	Uncertainty in wall depth estimation & difficulty observing paint conditions.
P0148	[List Paragraph]	VR Stereo
P0149	[List Paragraph]	Increased presence and awareness of the work area or boundary for some participants.
P0150	[List Paragraph]	Indirect or unfamiliar view, depth uncertainty and difficulty observing paint conditions.
P0151	[List Paragraph]	Monitor PCD
P0152	[List Paragraph]	Viewpoint adjustment was helpful for some participants.
P0153	[List Paragraph]	Adjusted views often did not reveal painting defects or surface-contact cues well.
P0154	[List Paragraph]	VR PCD
P0155	[List Paragraph]	Control burden within robot teleoperation and viewpoint adjustment.
P0156	[List Paragraph]	Unclear and annoying visuals (Point-cloud artifacts, robot occlusion, shadows) reduced the visibility of surface-state information.
P0157	[List Paragraph]	Overall Interpretation
P0158	[List Paragraph]	Monitor conditions produced fewer boundary-exceedance errors than VR, suggesting that a stable visual reference remained important for precise painting [42]
P0159	[List Paragraph]	Lower PCD Working Time Rates may reflect viewpoint-management effort that yielded limited task-relevant information [44]
P0160	[List Paragraph]	Task-Grounded Visual Requirements for Surface Treatment Teleoperation (RQ2; Table. 7)
P0161	[List Paragraph]	Operator need to monitor the evolving surface state while maintaining precise work within the work boundary.
P0162	[List Paragraph]	Painting-State and Defect Visibility
P0163	[List Paragraph]	Operators need to see not only where painting occurs, but also how the surface condition changes during execution.
P0164	[List Paragraph]	Global and Local Spatial Awareness
P0165	[List Paragraph]	Awareness of both the overall work boundary and the local EOAT–surface relationship.
P0166	[List Paragraph]	Visual Stability and Viewpoint Accessibility
P0167	[List Paragraph]	A stable visual reference helps operators maintain orientation during continuous manipulation.
P0168	[List Paragraph]	Together, these requirements define task-state observability: continuous awareness of the tool, work boundary, and changing surface condition, building on HRI awareness and viewpoint research [45–47].
P0169	[List Paragraph]	Implications for Visual Interface Design in Surface Treatment Tasks
P0170	[List Paragraph]	Surface-State-Preserving Visualization:
P0171	[List Paragraph]	Preserve the visual appearance cues that reveal changes in the treated surface.
P0172	[List Paragraph]	Combine geometric views with high-fidelity imagery or task-state highlighting rather than relying on geometry alone.
P0173	[List Paragraph]	Persistent Global–Local Visual Context
P0174	[List Paragraph]	Keep the target, boundary, tool, and local tool–surface interaction concurrently visible
P0175	[List Paragraph]	Overview-detail layouts, overlays, or equivalent spatial cues can reduce the need to trade global orientation for local inspection.
P0176	[List Paragraph]	Stable Primary View with Low-Cost Viewpoint Access:
P0177	[List Paragraph]	Maintain a stable primary view while enabling local inspection without disrupting orientation or control.
P0178	[List Paragraph]	Minimize the burden to re-establish the relationship between the tool, target region, and surface.
P0179	[List Paragraph]	Limitations and Future Work
P0180	[List Paragraph]	The study used a single laboratory geometry, material, EOAT, and task duration.
P0181	[List Paragraph]	Although the tested configurations used commercially available hardware and reflected visual approaches used in recent teleoperation research [6,27], the findings apply to these implementations rather than all VR or PCD systems.
P0182	[List Paragraph]	Future work should validate the visual requirements and proposed visual supports in varied painting conditions and related tasks.
P0183	[List Paragraph]	Conclusion
P0184	[List Paragraph]	Summary
P0185	[List Paragraph]	Summary of Quantitative & Qualitative results
P0186	[List Paragraph]	Three critical visual factors derived at discussion.
P0187	[List Paragraph]	Corresponding design implications.
P0188	[List Paragraph]	Contribution
P0189	[List Paragraph]	User-centered comparison of Workspace Representation and Display Modality in a construction-relevant surface-treatment teleoperation task, where comparative evidence on visual-interface design has remained limited.
P0190	[List Paragraph]	It contributes empirical evidence on how these visual-interface dimensions relate to task accuracy and operator experience. The findings are translated into task-grounded visual requirements for remote robotic painting.
P0191	[List Paragraph]	The findings can guide visual-interface evaluation and design for teleoperated surface treatment tasks in construction.

T6R2	Monitor Stereo	Stable and intuitive visual reference with residual depth uncertainty	Stable viewpoint supported target approach and boundary tracking, but distance and subtle defects remained uncertain.	“The monitor view was clear and more intuitive for approaching the target.” (S04)
T6R3	Monitor PCD	Flexible view exploration with limited surface detail	Adjusting viewpoint supported local inspection, but closer views did not reliably reveal painting quality or defects.	“Zooming in and scrolling left and right helped because the result was difficult to examine from a distance.” (S02)
T6R4	VR Stereo	Immersive Presence but Indirect Visual Reference	VR enhanced work-area awareness for some participants, but its indirect visual reference increased depth uncertainty and made precise correction demanding.	“The image was not fixed, which made it uncomfortable to work.” (S06)
T6R5	VR PCD	Competing demands of viewpoint management and robot control	Although movable viewpoints were useful, coordinating head movement, viewpoint adjustment, and robot control increased workload.	“I had to adjust the 3D viewpoint while simultaneously controlling the robot.” (S07)
